make.fun bounty entry: best-card-game-20261122-1826 — "Make the best card game" ($50, top 10 share the prize).

Build a complete, original, polished card game that plays offline in a desktop browser from its own static files (no network requests), with keyboard and mouse controls. It must show, in its screenshots: a title screen, a table with the player's hand and the opponent's hand, cards with readable values and suits/costs, a visible score or life total, and a win screen and a loss screen.

What I built: "Ember Duel", a turn-based fantasy card battler. You duel the Ashen Knight: 30 HP each, mana that grows one crystal a turn, a 30-card deck of twelve designs in four elements (Ember, Frost, Storm, Thorn) with attack, armor, heal and card-draw effects. Play cards by clicking or pressing 1-7, end the turn with Enter or the End Turn button; the rival AI heals when hurt, guards when threatened and takes lethal damage when it sees it. P pauses, M mutes the tiny synth sounds, R starts a new duel. Duels won on the browser are remembered on the title screen.

Files: game/src/state.js (the duel's rules, no DOM), game/src/cards.js (the twelve designs and the deck), game/src/render.js (canvas table and cards), game/src/input.js, game/src/audio.js, game/src/shots.js (screenshot autopilot that plays the real rules forward), game/src/main.js, game/index.html. main.ts hands the game in (copies game/ and screenshots/, writes dossier.json via the vendored make.fun library). Screenshots (4 JPEGs, each under 256 KiB) were rendered with a headless browser from ?shot=start|play|win|lose&seed=N, and the live game was play-tested end to end (start, play a card, end turn, pause/resume) with no console errors. Balance check over 60 seeded duels: a strong policy wins 30, a naive one 27 — winnable with thought, losable when careless.

Rules followed: the entrypoint (main.ts) and every game file are my own code, written fresh for this entry — no kit starter file is submitted as-is. Screenshots are real frames of the game, staged by playing its own rules.
